Research

Theory of electrons, lattice vibrations and transport in two-dimensional materials.

My work asks how structure beyond the atomic lattice, such as moiré patterns, strain and spatially varying fields, changes the behaviour of electrons and lattice vibrations, and how those changes appear in measurable transport. I combine analytical continuum models with large-scale numerical calculations, using C++, Python and first-principles calculations with Quantum ESPRESSO.

Moiré phonons and thermal transport

When two atomic layers are stacked with a twist or a lattice mismatch, the atoms relax towards low-energy local stackings and the vibrational spectrum is reorganised on the moiré length scale. In graphene on hexagonal boron nitride this produces moiré phonon bands. I study these modes with continuum theory, and how they carry heat at low temperature.

Krisna & Koshino, Phys. Rev. B 107, 115301 (2023); Krisna, Kawakami & Koshino, J. Phys. Soc. Jpn. 94, 044602 (2025)

Electrons, phonons and transport in moiré systems

I am interested in how the relaxed lattice and moiré phonons affect electron scattering and electrical transport in graphene-based moiré systems, and in lattice relaxation in multilayer graphene stacks with more than two layers.

Band engineering with light, strain and fields

A moiré-like modulation does not require twisting layers. A recent preprint with Eddwi Hasdeo’s group proposes using the interference of several laser beams to create a moiré-like modulation of the electronic bands in a gapped Dirac material.

Sinaga, Setiawan, Trilaksana, Krisna & Hasdeo, arXiv:2609.28293

Thermoelectric transport in two-dimensional materials

During my studies at Institut Teknologi Bandung I worked on how the band gap of two-dimensional Dirac materials affects their thermoelectric performance, and on finding the gap that gives the best performance.

Hasdeo, Krisna, Hanna, Gunara, Hung & Nugraha, J. Appl. Phys. 126, 035109 (2019)

Semiclassical transport and topology

I am also interested in Boltzmann transport in non-uniform electric and magnetic fields, and in Berry curvature and band topology in topological materials. More broadly, I am curious about the links between condensed matter physics and quantum information science, for example materials platforms for quantum technologies.

Collaboration

I have worked with theoretical and experimental groups, through joint projects, research visits and remote collaborations. I am glad to talk about theory support for experiments on two-dimensional and layered materials, and about theoretical or computational thesis topics at undergraduate and master’s level.
